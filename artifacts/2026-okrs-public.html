⚠ Critical Sequencing Rule: OKR 1 (Complete Docebo Reconstruction, guided by the Architecture Hub in OKR 0) is mutually dependent with OKR 3 (FZ onboarding inside the Regional menu) and OKR 4 (FZ dashboards in Insights). Time Allocation Rule of Thumb

Monday AM (1 hr): Weekly OKR check-in. Name the single most important deliverable for the week. Review FZ production pipeline status and Devin field-visit calendar separately.

Tue–Thu (2–3 hrs/day): Deep work blocks on OKR 1 (anchor). No meeting scheduling during these windows if possible. This is where the LMS build actually happens.

Friday (30 min): Log hours, update tracker, flag at-risk deliverables for next week. Chase external reviewers where needed.

Conference-protection rule: Between now and mid-September, treat every new-initiative request as a direct trade against OKR 1. If it has to be yes, name what slides. Don't compress the anchor.

Roadblocks & Risks

Mitigation Plans

The critical path runs: Governance Playbook → backend rebuilt → features activated → Rise build → Docebo deploy → pilot → conference. Each link has a failure mode that cascades downstream. These are the risks worth planning against now.

High Risk
⚠ IT / Docebo Platform Dependencies

Some architecture work requires IT or Docebo account-team involvement: menu creation at scale, group-scope permission changes, Notification Center configuration, Insights dashboard role access, any SSO-adjacent configuration. If IT request queues are long or Docebo support windows don't align, weeks get lost waiting.

Week 1 task: named meeting with IT and Docebo account team to walk through the LMS rebuild plan and get clarity on which changes can be made directly vs. which require their involvement. Flag the Notification Center configuration, Insights role access, and group-scope changes as the highest-dependency items. If IT queues slow, escalate to Sr. Director early — don't wait for the dependency to bite mid-July.
High Risk
⚠ July CTM Meeting Date — Immovable Anchor for OKR 9

The Monday Readiness Email and dashboard breakout launch are both gated on the July CTM meeting as the introduction vehicle. If the CTM meeting date shifts or agenda time is lost, the entire OKR 9 sequence misfires. Launching the email without the CTM introduction means announcing a tool cold to operators who haven't seen the dashboards yet.

Confirm CTM date and agenda slot by end of May — before OKR 9 build work intensifies. If the date moves, the email launch moves with it (that's fine). If agenda slot gets squeezed, negotiate for a full breakout rather than compressed time. Hard rule: don't launch the email without the CTM introduction first, even if the dashboards are technically ready.
Medium Risk
◆ Devin Operator Availability (May–Sep Field Partnerships)

Both Devin partnerships depend on operator schedules that aren't under our control. Store construction timelines slip. NRO dates move. Operators running into daily pressure cancel scheduled observation time. A single operator pulling out mid-cycle costs half the evidence the synthesis (KR 7.4) depends on.

Build the partnerships around both operators so one can carry if the other goes sideways. Log observations after every touchpoint — don't wait to synthesize at the end. If the NRO date shifts, treat that as an expected outcome rather than a crisis. Target 4 visits/quarter is achievable even with one operator rather than two.
Medium Risk
◆ New-Initiative Scope Creep Against the Anchor

Things will come up between now and conference — new programs, new dashboards, training requests. Each individually seems small. Three of them across May–August consume an entire KR's worth of hours. OKR 1 has no slack to absorb this.

Conference-protection rule: every new-initiative request between now and mid-September is treated as a direct trade against OKR 1. If it has to be yes, name what slides. "Yes, but the backend rebuild moves to July" is a real answer. Sr. Director alignment on this rule before the first ask arrives — so the pushback has cover.
Medium Risk
◆ Automation and AI Workflow Adoption

Automations #1 and #2 produce reports that have to actually be looked at for the governance calendar to work. AI workflows only recover hours if they get used. Building them without adoption means the hours-recovered KR (6.3) fails — and the redirected OKR 5 capacity disappears with it.

SOP docs for each automation + AI workflow aren't end-state — they're the onboarding artifact. Each automation includes named owner (who gets the weekly email), clear trigger action, escalation point. AI workflows documented end-to-end so anyone can run them independently without tacit knowledge transfer.
Low Risk
✓ ILT Anchor Conversation Timing (VP Ops + RDO Availability)

KR 5.4 requires one 60-minute session with VP of Ops and 2–3 RDOs between early November and mid-December. End-of-year schedules are volatile. A single failed scheduling attempt could push to January — which breaks the "60 days of post-launch data" input premise.

Pre-flag the conversation at conference (W23) — "I'd like to bring you data from the new LMS in Nov or Dec; is your November open?" Calendar request goes out end of October. If December slips, January is acceptable — the anchor conversation's value doesn't evaporate with a month's delay. The only hard deadline is before 2027 ILT OKR work begins.

Execution Strategies

How to Protect the Plan

Six operating principles that run across the whole plan — patterns to apply recurrently, not one-time decisions.

🎯

Stack Compound Work

  • One hour, two deliverables. Every OKR 1 task should produce at least one reusable artifact.
  • AI workflow outputs feed OKR 1 activations. Workflow #1 (metadata tagging) → OKR 1.4 Skill Management. Workflow #2 (checklist generation) → OKR 1.4 Observation Checklists. No standalone AI experiments.
  • Documentation is a byproduct, not a separate task. SOPs for automations are written while building, not as a later writing sprint.
🛡

Protect Deep Work Blocks May–August

  • Tuesday through Thursday, 2–3 hours/day, blocked for OKR 1 anchor work.
  • Monday morning (1 hr) is check-in only. Review production pipeline status, update tracker, confirm week's single most important deliverable.
  • Friday afternoon (30 min) is close-out. Log hours, flag at-risk items, chase external reviewers.
🗓

One Priority Per Week

  • Every week names its single most important deliverable. That deliverable drives the deep-work blocks.
  • If the week's priority slips, nothing else advances until it's back on track. Stops cascading delays.
  • Published in Smartsheet. Visible to Sr. Director.
📊

Use the Data Being Built

  • Insights dashboards replace intuition. Decisions about what to iterate post-launch come from signal data, not anecdote.
  • Governance calendar produces weekly inputs. Default Menu audit, catalog orphan report, courses-not-accessed.
  • Friction report + dashboard gap report are scoped as OKR 5 deliverables, not optional side analyses.
🤝

Manage Dependencies Proactively

  • Named reviewer per packet, specific turnaround date, midpoint check-in, escalation path documented.
  • External dependency dashboard updated weekly. Which reviews are in flight, which are late.
  • Chase early, not late. A 1-week slip caught at week 2 costs an afternoon; caught at week 4 costs a month.
✂

Ruthless Scope Discipline

  • Conference-protection rule through September. Every new-initiative request is a trade against OKR 1.
  • Post-launch v2 list exists for a reason. Items surfacing mid-build that aren't critical go to the v2 list, not into the current scope.
  • "Nice to have" is not on this plan. The plan is the minimum viable conference launch.
