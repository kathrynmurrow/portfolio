Origin

Built because feedback without evidence isn't feedback — it's opinion.

01 The problem

Coaching was happening but nothing was changing

School leaders were receiving feedback from principals and coaches, but the feedback wasn't grounded in observed behavior. Without standards mastery, low-inference data, or accountability structures, the conversations felt productive and produced nothing. The cycle repeated every year.

02 The design decision

Evidence before judgment. Always.

The framework was built around a single non-negotiable: feedback must be grounded in observable, standards-aligned evidence before it's delivered. The four steps weren't arbitrary — they created the conditions for feedback that could actually be acted on, not just heard and forgotten.

03 The collaboration

Co-designed with the Chief Schools Officer

The framework wasn't designed in isolation — it was built in partnership with the district's Chief Schools Officer and deployed through a cross-functional system for 350+ instructional leaders and coaches. The principal coaching system that emerged from it produced a 77% school improvement rate.

04 The lineage

The direct precursor to CLEAR coaching

Every element of this framework — observable evidence, structured analysis, differentiated feedback, accountable follow-up — now lives inside the CLEAR coaching model embedded in the Bojangles Leadership Foundations Series. The context changed. The structure held.

The framework

Four steps. Evidence at every stage.

01
↓
Step one

Attain knowledge of the standards

Before any observation can produce credible feedback, the coach must deeply understand what excellent practice looks like — the standards, the vertical alignment, the specific indicators of mastery at each level. Without this foundation, observation is just watching.

How it's done
  • PLC participation and presence
  • See/hear chart development
  • Vertical alignment of standards across grade levels
  • Content-specific professional learning
Evidence of completion

Coach can articulate what "good" looks like in observable terms before entering a classroom.

02
↓
Step two

Conduct classroom observations

Observations must produce low-inference data — what was seen and heard, not what was interpreted. The use of calibrated observation roles and see/hear charts ensures that what gets recorded is evidence, not judgment. This is the step most coaching systems skip.

How it's done
  • Defined observation roles (scripting, tallying, scanning)
  • Low-inference data collection — behavior, not evaluation
  • See/hear charts to separate observation from interpretation
  • Inter-rater calibration for consistency across coaches
Evidence of completion

Coach has documented behavioral data that could be verified by another observer in the same room.

03
↓
Step three

Analyze data and determine area of focus

The data from multiple observations is synthesized to identify patterns — not isolated moments. A data-driven area of focus is selected based on what the evidence actually shows, not what the coach already believed. Cumulative evidence prevents recency bias from shaping the coaching conversation.

How it's done
  • Synthesis of findings across multiple observations
  • Data-driven identification of the area of focus
  • Cumulative coaching evidence review
  • Pattern identification across sessions
Evidence of completion

A single, specific area of focus selected from evidence — not assumption — with data to support it.

04
Step four

Deliver standards-based feedback

Feedback is delivered with specific, actionable next steps grounded in the evidence collected — not general encouragement or vague direction. Accountability is built into the delivery: follow-up is scheduled, documentation is maintained, and the loop closes at the next observation cycle.

How it's done
  • Evidence-aligned, actionable next steps
  • Differentiated delivery based on leader's development stage
  • Accountability through scheduled follow-up
  • Coaching documentation maintained across cycles
Evidence of completion

Leader can articulate one specific next behavior and the date of the follow-up conversation.
